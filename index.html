Research

Learned representations of fNIRS for functional brain mapping Huppert Lab

We project fNIRS probe signals into the standard 10–20 scalp space and train CNNs to predict which function each activated brain region is performing. The goal is portable, at-home brain imaging with accuracy close to the lab.

Tasks and research questions

Functions: motor (left and right hand, left and right foot, tongue), language (math versus story), social cognition, and working memory (n-back).

  • Cross-subject: does a model trained on some people predict function in new people?
  • Cross-task: after training on four cognitive tasks, can it predict the next four?
  • Transfer: is it better to pretrain on ImageNet, then fMRI, then fNIRS, or to train on fNIRS directly?

Conformational bias in RNA force fields Nagan Lab, URECA

Comparing torsion distributions across thousands of molecular dynamics frames to detect conformational biases between force fields and solvent models, with the aim of refining their parameters. Porting the analysis to CUDA is in progress; poster planned for spring 2027.

Smoothing methods for nonsmooth optimization With Dr. Flynn, BNL

Planned for summer 2027: the open questions on rate-tracking smoothing raised in my talk for Dr. Flynn's group, including when shrinking the smoothing radius during training guarantees convergence to Clarke-stationary points.
